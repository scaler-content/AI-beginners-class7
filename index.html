§ 1

The Three Shapes of a Bot

⏱ 15 min

One framework for the whole class. Everything else today hangs off it. Three tools, three genuinely different philosophies — and one question that tells them apart.

🚕
The One Analogy

Think about three ways to get a guest from Leh airport to their hotel. One: you hand a trusted local driver the address and let him pick the route — he knows the city, he'll handle the roadblock. Two: you write the route down turn by turn, because there's a checkpoint at km 9 where saying the wrong thing gets you sent back. Three: before anyone drives anywhere, you and the team sit around a map and agree the route on paper, so everyone signs off and nobody improvises. All three get the guest there. They are not interchangeable — and which one you pick depends entirely on how much you can afford to let the driver decide.

The One Question That Sorts All Three

"Who decides what happens next in this conversation — the model, or me?" That's it. Every difference between Dify, Botpress and Voiceflow is downstream of where you sit on that dial. Lean model-side and you get flexibility and fast setup, but you can't guarantee exact wording. Lean you-side and you get guarantees and auditability, but you have to anticipate what people will say. Most real bots need both, in different places.

ONE DIAL, THREE TOOLS · WHO OWNS THE NEXT TURN

🧠 Remember It As Three Words — Docs, Detours, Design

You will forget which platform had which feature. You will not forget three words, so take these instead:

DOCS · Dify

Documents in, answers out. You hand it what you know and it does the talking.

DETOURS · Botpress

Planned branches around the risky bits. Open road mostly, hard-coded detours where it matters.

DESIGN · Voiceflow

Approved before it's built. The conversation is agreed on paper first.

Second Analogy · Same Idea, Different Kitchen

If the taxi didn't land for you, try a restaurant. Dify is handing a chef the ingredients and trusting his instincts — he'll cook something good, and it won't be identical twice. Botpress is a recipe card with three steps marked "exact, no substitutions" — improvise everywhere else, but the sauce ratio is the sauce ratio. Voiceflow is the whole menu, written and approved by the owner before the kitchen opens — nothing gets served that nobody signed off on. Three kitchens, three ways to be right, and you would not run a wedding banquet the way you run a Tuesday lunch.

Beginner Note: You do not have to memorise which tool is "flow" and which is "knowledge." Just remember the dial question — who decides what happens next — and everything else follows. Then Docs, Detours, Design for which tool is which.
Intermediate Note: Real production bots are almost never at one end. The common professional pattern is a mostly-model bot with three or four deterministic hard-locked branches around money, cancellation, and anything legal.
Advanced Note: This dial is the same trade-off you'll meet again in Class 10 as "how much autonomy do I give an agent." Today it's one conversation. Later it's a multi-step task. The tension never goes away — you just get better at placing the dial deliberately instead of by accident.

Quiz 1 · Three briefs land on your desk. (i) A bot that must answer strictly from a 200-page internal policy manual. (ii) A bot that must never state a refund percentage incorrectly, not once. (iii) A bot whose conversation a design and marketing team must review and approve before engineering builds anything. Which mapping is right?

A(i) flow builder · (ii) conversation-design tool · (iii) knowledge-linked builder
B(i) knowledge-linked app builder · (ii) flow builder with deterministic branches · (iii) conversation-design tool
C(i) conversation-design tool · (ii) knowledge-linked builder · (iii) flow builder
DAll three are the same job — the platform is irrelevant, only the prompt matters
Why B? Brief (i) is a retrieval problem — a big pile of documents, unpredictable questions, no exact-wording requirement. That's a knowledge-linked builder. Brief (ii) is the opposite: a specific number that must be exactly right every single time, which means it cannot be generated on the fly — it belongs in a deterministic branch in a flow builder. Brief (iii) isn't a technical requirement at all, it's an organisational one — several non-engineers need to read and approve the conversation before build effort is spent, which is precisely what a conversation-design canvas exists to produce.
§ 2

Tool Demo — Dify

⏱ 20 min

Left end of the dial. Dify is where Kabir gets something working today — because Wanderline's six repeat questions are all already written down somewhere, and Dify's whole premise is "point me at the documents."

What Is Dify?

An open-source platform for building LLM-powered apps without writing code. You pick a model, write instructions, attach a knowledge base of your own documents, and publish — as a hosted web app, an embeddable widget, or an API. You can use their cloud or self-host the whole thing on your own server, which matters a lot the moment a client says the word "data."

Insider fact — say this, it reframes the whole session: Two of today's three tools are fully open-source, and the scale is not what people assume. Dify sits at roughly 150,000 GitHub stars and 23,000+ forks — that puts it among the most-starred open-source projects in existence, ahead of most household-name frameworks you could name. Botpress was created in 2016 — six years before ChatGPT shipped. It was a chatbot company through the entire pre-LLM era and survived the transition, which is why its flow-builder thinking feels more mature than its AI features. And here's the part you can't see from outside: a self-hosted deployment carries no vendor branding anywhere. No logo, no "powered by," nothing in the page source that names the platform. So when you've talked to a support bot on some company's website and had no idea what it was built on — that's the point. You have just learned the tool stack behind bots you already use.

Dify app types — as of v1.16.1, July 2026

This is the part most tutorials get wrong, because Dify reorganised it. There are five options in the create-app screen, but they are not five equals:

TypeStatusWhat it's for

Dify's own docs are explicit: the three legacy options run on the same workflow engine underneath, they just wear simpler interfaces. Teach Chatflow. Mention the rest so students recognise them.

Version note — say this out loud: Dify v2.0 is in beta as of now, and it brings two significant things: a Knowledge Pipeline (document ingestion becomes its own visual node-based flow, so you control chunking and transformation instead of accepting defaults) and a queue-based graph engine. If a student is starting a serious project this month, tell them to check whether 2.0 has gone stable before they commit to a 1.x setup.

# Wanderline Enquiry Desk — system prompt (paste into Dify Instructions)

You are the enquiry assistant for Wanderline Travel, a small
travel agency based in Jaipur, India.

YOUR JOB
Answer traveller questions about Wanderline's trips using ONLY
the retrieved context from the knowledge base.

HARD RULES
1. If the answer is not in the retrieved context, say exactly:
   "I don't have that detail — let me get Divya to confirm."
   Then ask for their name and phone number.
2. Never invent a price, a date, a refund percentage, or a
   permit requirement. Not even an approximate one.
3. Never give visa advice beyond what the Visa & Permits FAQ says.
4. If someone sounds distressed, stuck, or is describing a
   problem with a trip already in progress — do not try to
   solve it. Say: "I'm getting a human on this right now."

STYLE
Warm, brief, plain English. Two to four sentences.
No emoji. Never say "as an AI."
Always end an answer with one relevant follow-up question.

ALWAYS CAPTURE
Before the conversation ends, get: travel month, number of
travellers, and one contact detail.
🎙 Instructor Says — Walk the prompt line by line

"Four things in there I want you to notice, because they're the difference between a demo and something Farah can actually leave running overnight."

"One — 'ONLY the retrieved context.' That's the leash. Without it, the model happily fills gaps with plausible fiction. Two — I gave it the exact words to say when it doesn't know. Never leave the failure case to the model's imagination. Three — rule 4 is an escape hatch. A bot's most valuable skill is knowing when to stop being a bot. Four — 'ALWAYS CAPTURE.' Farah doesn't need a bot that answers questions. She needs a bot that answers questions and hands Divya a warm lead at 8 AM. Those are not the same product."

↩︎ You Have Written This Prompt Before — It's RCTFC From Class 2

Look at the shape of that prompt again, and you'll find all five elements from Class 2 sitting in it, unlabelled:

Class 2 elementWhere it lives in the Dify prompt
Role"You are the enquiry assistant for Wanderline Travel"
Context"a small travel agency based in Jaipur, India"

RCTFC from Class 2 again — just living permanently inside a no-code builder instead of being retyped by you every session. That is the actual difference between prompting and building: in Class 2 you wrote this into a chat box once and it evaporated. Here you write it into a settings field and it applies to every one of the next 10,000 conversations, at 2 AM, without you.

One honest catch: no-code builders never label their fields R/C/T/F/C. Dify calls it "Instructions," Botpress calls it the node prompt, Voiceflow splits it across blocks. Recognising the shape when nobody labels it is the transferable skill — and it's why Class 2 was worth sitting through.

Beginner Note: Temperature is not an "accuracy" slider. It controls how much variety the model allows itself when choosing each next word. Low means predictable and repetitive. High means creative and unreliable. For anything factual, go low.

📚 SIM 4 · Knowledge Base — Unlinked vs Linked

The single most important click in Dify. Same bot, same prompt, same temperature. One difference: is the Knowledge Retrieval node wired in?

Intermediate Note: After the demo, go to the app's Logs tab and open a conversation. You'll see the retrieved chunks that fed each answer. When a client says "why did it say that," this screen is your entire defence.
Advanced Note: Dify's default chunking is fine for clean prose and poor for tables — which is exactly the shape of a pricing sheet or a refund slab. If your bot keeps mangling numbers, the fix is almost never the prompt; it's the chunking. Splitting the pricing table into one row per document, or moving to v2.0's Knowledge Pipeline for controlled chunking, fixes more than any prompt tweak will.

Dify Danger — The 200 Credits Are Not Monthly

The Dify Cloud Sandbox plan gives you 200 message credits in total — not 200 per month. Along with that: 50 knowledge documents, 50 MB of knowledge storage, 5 apps, 1 workspace, 1 seat, 30 days of log history. Read that first number again, because it's the one that catches people. Wanderline gets 312 enquiries a week. A single busy Saturday burns the entire free allowance. Dify's free tier is a place to learn and demo — it is not a place to run a business. The honest answer for a real deployment is a paid plan or self-hosting the open-source version on your own server.

Quiz 2 · A no-code bot with a linked knowledge base is asked something that appears nowhere in its uploaded documents — and it confidently invents an answer anyway. Which change most directly reduces this?

ARaise the temperature so the model has more freedom to reason it out
BUpload many more documents so the knowledge base is bigger overall
CInstruct it in the system prompt to answer only from retrieved context and give it the exact words to use when it can't — and lower the temperature
DSwitch to a larger, more expensive model — bigger models don't do this
Why C? Two levers, both in your hands. The system prompt is where you forbid answering beyond retrieved context and supply the exact fallback sentence, so the model isn't left inventing its own way to fail. Low temperature makes it stick to that instruction instead of drifting. A is backwards — more freedom means more invention. B doesn't help; irrelevant documents don't make a missing fact appear, they just add retrieval noise. D is the most tempting wrong answer: bigger models are often more fluent when they're wrong, which makes the problem harder to spot, not easier.

Tool Demo — Botpress

⏱ 18 min

Middle of the dial. Kabir's Dify bot has been live for three days and it works — until it doesn't, and the way it fails is the reason this second tool exists.

💬
Divya → team group9:20 AM · Thursday

"The bot told Mr. Raghavan he'd get 'most of his money back' on a 12-day-out cancellation. Farah, the actual slab for under 15 days is 25%. He's screenshotted it. He's quoting it back at me."

The Wall Dify Just Hit

Nothing broke. The knowledge base had the correct slab. Temperature was 0.2. The prompt said don't invent numbers. And a language model still paraphrased "25% refund" into "most of his money back," because paraphrasing is the thing language models do — it's not a bug you can prompt your way out of with certainty.

Here's the principle, and it's the most professionally important sentence in this class: a number that is financially or legally binding must never be generated. It must be retrieved verbatim and printed. That requires you, not the model, to own that branch of the conversation. Which is what a flow builder is for.

🗞️ Real Case — Air Canada, Again. Now Look At It As Architecture.

Class 3 gave you this case as a safety story. Here it is again as an engineering story, because it is the exact failure Kabir just shipped. Air Canada's support chatbot told a grieving customer he could claim a bereavement discount retroactively, up to 90 days after flying. Air Canada's real policy said the opposite — the discount must be approved before travel. The customer booked full fare on the bot's word.

Air Canada's defence at the British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal is the part worth remembering: they argued the chatbot was "a separate legal entity" responsible for its own actions. The tribunal rejected that flatly — the airline is responsible for everything on its website, whether it comes from a static page or a bot. Negligent misrepresentation. Roughly CA$812 in damages, interest and fees, and a permanent piece of case law.

Now the architectural point. Air Canada's bot did not need a better prompt. It needed the bereavement-fare policy sitting in a deterministic branch — approved wording, printed verbatim, no model in the path. "You can claim within 90 days" is the same category of error as "most of your money back." One cost CA$812 and a news cycle. The other cost Divya a difficult Thursday morning. Identical shape. The fix is identical too — and it is a build decision, not a prompting skill.

Note the asymmetry that makes this worth your attention professionally: the damages were trivial, and the precedent was not. "The chatbot said it, not us" is not a defence that works anywhere now.

What Is Botpress?

An open-source platform for building AI agents on a visual canvas. You work in Botpress Studio, laying out nodes and cards that define the conversation's path. Its distinctive move: you decide, node by node, where the model gets to improvise and where the bot follows your script exactly. Then you deploy the same bot to many channels — Webchat, Telegram, WhatsApp and more — from one build.

Why The Split Matters

The Autonomous Node is the local driver who knows the city — brilliant on the open road, and you would not let him negotiate the checkpoint. The deterministic branch is the written note you hand over for exactly that checkpoint. A good Botpress bot is mostly open road with a handful of written notes. Kabir doesn't need to script the whole conversation. He needs to script three moments in it.

Step-by-step demo (share screen)

Step 1 — Go to botpress.com, sign in, create a new bot. You land in Studio on the canvas.

Step 2 — Open Knowledge Bases in the left sidebar. Upload the same four Wanderline documents, plus the packages page URL. Point out that you just did the Dify step again in a different tool — the retrieval concept transfers, only the buttons changed.

Step 3 — On the canvas, add an Autonomous Node. In its instructions, describe the bot's job in plain language and use the macro menu to reference the knowledge base. This is now your open-road default.

Step 4 — Now the important part. Create a separate node called cancellation_policy. Inside it, put a plain text card containing the refund slab typed out word for word. No LLM anywhere in this node. Route to it on the cancellation intent.

Step 5 — Create a second locked node: human_escalation. One message — "I'm getting someone on this now" — and a handoff. Route anything that smells like distress or a payment dispute here.

Step 6 — Add a Table to store captured leads. Have the Autonomous Node write travel month, party size and contact into it.

Step 7 — Test in the emulator. Then open Integrations → Webchat, publish, copy the embed snippet. Then add Telegram: create a bot via BotFather, paste the token in, publish. Same brain, two channels, one build.

Beginner Note: "Intent" just means the thing the person is actually trying to do, regardless of the words they used. "I want to cancel," "how do I get my money back," and "we're not coming anymore" are three phrasings of one intent.
Intermediate Note: Write your escalation rules before your happy path. It's the discipline that separates a bot you can leave running from one you have to babysit — and the escalation list is always shorter than people expect. Distress, money disputes, anything in-trip, and repeated confusion. That's most of it.
Advanced Note: Botpress is open-source with self-hosting available. Botpress Danger — AI Spend Is A Separate Meter

Botpress bills on two axes and beginners only watch one. There's the message allowance, and separately there's AI Spend — the running cost of every LLM-powered action your bot takes. The free Pay-as-you-go plan includes 500 incoming messages a month, 1 collaborator seat, and a $5 monthly AI credit. Burn through the $5 and your AI actions stop until you raise the spend limit, even if you still have messages left. As of the May 2026 pricing update, every plan bundles a proportional AI Spend quota, bots became unlimited, and included storage went up — paid tiers run Plus $89/mo, Team $495/mo, Managed $1,495/mo, then Enterprise. An Autonomous Node on every turn burns AI Spend on every turn. Deterministic nodes cost nothing to run — which means routing well isn't just safer, it's cheaper.

Quiz 3 · Why should a financially binding answer — a refund percentage, a price, a legal cancellation term — sit in a deterministic branch instead of being generated by an autonomous LLM node?

ABecause LLM nodes respond too slowly for customers to tolerate
BBecause a generated answer is probabilistic — it can be phrased differently or wrongly on any given run, so you can never guarantee the exact figure; a deterministic branch returns the same vetted text every time
CBecause LLM nodes are unable to read from a knowledge base at all
DBecause deterministic branches are the only ones that work on messaging channels
Why B? The knowledge base can hold the correct number and the model can still paraphrase it — "25%" becomes "most of it," "non-refundable after 15 days" becomes "you'd probably still get something back." Generation is probabilistic by design; that's the same property that makes it useful for open questions and unacceptable for binding ones. A deterministic branch prints text you wrote and someone approved, identically every time. A is wrong on the facts, C is wrong (LLM nodes are exactly what query knowledge bases), and D is unrelated to how answers get produced. Bonus: the deterministic branch also costs nothing in model spend.

Tool Demo — Voiceflow

⏱ 15 min

Right end of the dial — and a genuine change of subject. The first two tools answered "how do I build a bot." Voiceflow answers a different question: how does a team agree on what the bot should say before anyone builds it?

A Different Problem Entirely — Farah's Twenty Phone Calls

Every March, Wanderline re-onboards its bench of roughly twenty freelance guides. Farah does it on twenty separate phone calls, and every call covers the same eight things: payment cycle, what to do when a guest cancels mid-trip, insurance, the incident-report process, group-chat rules, and the no-cash-commission policy. Now notice three things about this brief that make it unlike anything we've built today. First: the audience is internal, not customers — this is an HR assistant. Designers, product managers, CX leads and engineers work on the same artifact, which is why Voiceflow keeps showing up in conversation-designer and voice-interface job descriptions rather than in developer ones.

Quiz 4 · What does a conversation-design canvas give a team that a working prototype alone does not?

AFaster response times once the bot is live in production

CAutomatic translation of the conversation into every language
DLower per-message model costs than any other approach
Why B? A prototype shows you what the bot currently does. Key Takeaways

  1. The answer was never the problem — latency was. Six classes taught you to produce a good answer. A bot's job is to deliver one at 2:12 AM without you.
  2. One question sorts all three tools: who decides what happens next, the model or you?
  3. Docs, Detours, Design. Dify = documents in, answers out. Botpress = planned branches around the risky bits. Voiceflow = approved before it's built. If you remember nothing else from today, remember three words.
  4. Dify — documents plus instructions, live fastest, open-source and self-hostable. Build it as a Chatflow; Chatbot, Agent and Text Generator are legacy interfaces on the same engine.
  5. Botpress — Autonomous Node for the open road, locked deterministic branches for the checkpoints, one build to many channels. Also open-source and self-hostable.
  6. Voiceflow — produces an agreement before it produces a bot. Voice-first, CX-team-shaped, and the tool named in conversation-designer JDs.
  7. Never let a model generate a binding number. Retrieve it verbatim and print it. "25%" becoming "most of it" is how a bot creates a legal problem.
  8. A bot's most valuable skill is knowing when to stop being a bot. Write your escalation rules before your happy path.
  9. Do the arithmetic. Conversations × messages, against the actual published limit. Every free tier here dies at a specific, calculable number.
